Kilimo, Nitapata?

A citation-backed status check for Kenya's fertilizer e‑Voucher scheme, and the leverage that makes it worth running past today.

Why this needs to exist

He can't tell a fair price from an inflated one.

He already knows he's enrolled. What he doesn't know, until he's standing at the counter, is whether the price he's quoted is real, or whether he's about to lose a day's labour and a fare finding out the hard way.

One farmer reached his depot by 4am and still found seven people ahead of him. The Star, 25 Aug 2026
How it decides

Never guessed. Always cited.

Available · Utapata

A deterministic rule engine checks the farmer's own record against the depot's authorisation. No model inference in the verdict, ever.

Unavailable · Hutapata

Every requirement, gate, and price traces to a named, dated source. Where sources disagree, both figures are shown, never one asserted as correct.

Built to survive contact with the person who actually decides: the depot clerk with his own register and his own incentive to say no.

This is the sustainability story

One release. Every farmer. Same moment.

1:1,800

7.2M
Farmers already registered on KIAMIS, Nov 2025

A sub-county officer opens a release window. The same engine runs every farmer on her list. One press pushes status, price, and blocking item to everyone, in-app, SMS, WhatsApp, from one shared record. USSD stays available any time, for farmers checking on their own.

What it actually costs to run

A few hundred shillings, total, per release.

  • Under ten dollars per release, for every farmer on an officer's list, on the channels we control the cost of: SMS and WhatsApp, roughly KES 0.50 to 1.50 a farmer.
  • USSD is free for us to run, but the session itself costs the farmer a few shillings on his own airtime, unless we buy the toll-free line. That trade-off is a design choice we're naming, not hiding.
  • Precedent already exists: Nakuru county runs a digital agriculture platform in a formal partnership with CARE Kenya and Digital Green, government-plus-NGO co-delivery of exactly this pattern.

Cheaper than one wasted trip, many times over. And the subsidy budget itself was cut from $77M to $61M this year, a shrinking pool makes "can he trust the number" sharper, not softer.

Five people in this chain. One of them pays.

A few hundred shillings buys five kinds of value.

Farmer

Knows before he travels. Holds a citation at the counter.

Pays: no

Proxy

Relays a verified answer, not a guess, to the family.

Pays: no

Depot clerk

Not a user. The record he can't argue with.

Pays: no

Extension officer

1,800 farmers reached in one press, not 1,800 conversations.

Pays: no

County / NGO partner

A cheap, low-risk upgrade to extension delivery. Precedent already exists.

Pays: yes

The farmer is the one person in this chain who structurally cannot absorb another cost, that's the whole reason this exists. The ask sits inside a budget line the payer already has, not a new one.

The one thing we haven't tested

A system that timestamps her every action.

We haven't asked a real extension officer yet whether that's a convenience or a liability to her specifically. When a scheme misfires today, ambiguity protects the official from blame. This tool removes that ambiguity.

"If every farmer on your list could see what you see, at the same time you see it, would that make your job easier, or harder?"

That question, not a funding round, is the first real test.

What this deliberately is not

Discipline is the differentiator.

Not a marketplace Not agronomic advice Not a payment step Not live KIAMIS integration

Each exclusion is a stated choice, not a gap we're hoping nobody asks about. In a room full of hackathon demos, that discipline, five cited sources, an honest "link pending" where one isn't verified, no model ever guessing a verdict, is itself the case study.

The sharpened value proposition

One officer's release reaches 1,800 farmers who all see the same number, at the same time, before anyone at the counter gets a chance to change the story.
