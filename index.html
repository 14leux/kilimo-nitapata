Behind the answer

Every requirement

Price, as reported

Two sources report different capped prices, a year apart. Treat both as reported figures, not a guarantee. The depot's posted price on the day governs.

Sources

How this tool decides

The six requirements and the two prices are transcribed from the five published news and analysis sources above, not from the underlying gazette notice or the KIAMIS system's own user manual, which were not publicly retrievable at build time. Where sources disagree, both figures are shown with their date and source rather than one being picked as correct.

The match between a farmer and a depot is decided by a fixed rule table, not a language model: each requirement is checked against the selected farmer's record, each depot carries its own authorisation flag, and the verdict is whichever is worse of the two. Nothing is inferred or guessed.

Why this needs to exist

He can't tell a fair price from an inflated one.

He already knows he's enrolled. What he doesn't know, until he's standing at the counter, is whether the price he's being asked for is the real one, or whether he's about to lose a day's labour and a fare finding out the hard way that something in his registration is missing.

One farmer reached his depot by 4am and still found seven people ahead of him.

The Star, 25 Aug 2026

Knowing you're eligible doesn't shorten that queue. But knowing before you travel, and being able to prove the price at the counter, changes what the trip costs you if it goes wrong.

The person typing the question, the person deciding whether to travel, and the person who has to be convinced at the counter are three different people. Most tools like this get built for the first one and quietly fail the other two.

The farmer

Nikienda leo, nitapata? If I go today, will I get it? He doesn't think in terms of eligibility. He thinks in terms of "they said we'd get fertiliser," and finds out otherwise only after he's already spent the trip.

The proxy

He's the one actually holding the phone, usually a son, daughter or grandchild who already runs the household's WhatsApp. He has to relay the answer accurately enough, by voice, that an older relative is willing to travel on the strength of it.

The depot clerk

Not a user of this app. The person the answer has to survive. A farmer holding up a screen with a specific number, from a specific dated source, either corrects a mistake or starts a confrontation, and he's the one who absorbs it either way.

The extension officer

She's already answering this question, one farmer at a time, all day. If this tool makes her faster, adoption doesn't happen one download at a time. It happens in groups, the moment she uses it in front of people she already talks to.

What this is

A status check for the fertilizer subsidy e-Voucher scheme: whether a given depot will serve a given registered farmer, exactly what is still missing, and what the bag is documented to cost. Each answer is footnoted to the source it came from.
